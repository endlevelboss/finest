Pairing these two stories makes sense on paper. Both came out in 1987,
both retold Batman's early career after *Crisis*, and both give the
Dark Knight his start. Read together, though, they show how rarely a
story like *Year One* comes along.

## Year One

Frank Miller and David Mazzucchelli's four issues (*Batman* #404–407)
are still the benchmark. Miller writes in a clipped, noir style, but
the story he tells is surprisingly grounded. The book follows Bruce
Wayne's clumsy first nights out and James Gordon's arrival in a Gotham
police department rotten to the core. Gordon is really the lead: an
honest cop who isn't a saint, with a pregnant wife at home and a
corrupt commissioner breathing down his neck.

Mazzucchelli's art does most of the heavy lifting. His lines are
spare, the layouts are restrained, and he never wastes a panel. Richmond
Lewis's muted colors make Gotham feel cold, dirty and lived-in. The
standout scene is the tenement siege: Batman trapped, a SWAT team
closing in, and a swarm of bats called down to save him. It's as good
as superhero comics get.

## Year Two

Mike W. Barr's follow-up (*Detective Comics* #575–578) is a different
beast. Batman runs into the Reaper, a vigilante from Gotham's past who
kills the criminals he hunts, and to stop him he reluctantly teams up
with Joe Chill, the man who murdered his parents. He even picks up a
gun. The premise is bold, and the moral knot at its center is
genuinely interesting.

The execution is uneven. Alan Davis draws the first chapter
beautifully before Todd McFarlane takes over, and the shift in style
is jarring. The romance with Rachel Caspian never quite convinces, and
the plot leans on coincidence to bring everything together.
It's a solid, entertaining Batman story that suffers mostly from where
it sits: right after one of the best.

## Verdict

*Year One* alone makes this collection essential; *Year Two* is a
worthwhile curiosity that shows how hard that act was to follow. If
you only know the post-*Crisis* Batman from what came later, start
here.
